## Introduction to the collation of _Aphra Behn_ (1849) and _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof_ (1859), with initial findings
### Sophie van den Elzen and Marie Léger-St-Jean, August 2026

Publisher Otto Janke reissued Luise Mühlbach’s socially engaged historical novel _Aphra Behn_ (1849) as a “_[z]weite, neu bearbeitete Ausgabe_” (second, newly reworked edition) under the title _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof_ (1859). The text was revised throughout, with nearly all chapters receiving some updates, ranging from cutting an adjective to, most commonly, tweaking, reducing or cutting altogether character’s speeches.

### Method
In order to study the revisions to _Aphra Behn_ leading to _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof_, we found digital surrogates in HathiTrust and in the digital library of the Münchener DigitalisierungsZentrum (MDZ). The MDZ contained the digital surrogates of two copies of _Aphra Behn_, both scanned from the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, while HathiTrust gave access to the digital surrogate of _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof_ held in the University of Chicago’s Lincke Collection. We requested the images converted into text using optical character recognition (OCR) from MDZ and downloaded it from HathiTrust.[^note] We therefore had a total of nine text files: one for each volume of the two copies of the 1849 edition and the only copy of the 1859 edition.

[^note]: Scholars who are not affiliated to a HathiTrust member institution can download the electronic publication file (.epub) from Google Books and convert it to a text file (.txt) using the open-source ebook management software [calibre](https://calibre-ebook.com/).

The OCR’ed text did not only ease the burden of reading blackletter script (Fraktur), but allowed for automated forms of collation, or comparison of two or more textual variants. To do so, we used the digital tool Locate, Explore, Retrace and Apprehend complex text variants, or [LERA](https://lera.uzi.uni-halle.de/), developed by Marcus Pöckelmann and his colleagues. We uploaded each text file as a document into LERA, asking for them to be segmented at punctuation marks. This process created 7,800 to 8,200 segments for volume 1 (Book 1); 9,600 to 11,000 segments for volume 2 (Book 2); and 8,700 to 10,000 segments for volume 3 (Book 3).

We then created an edition for each volume with the three associated documents (i.e., the uploaded text files of the two copies of _Aphra Behn_ and the single copy of _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof_). LERA would then align segments of each of the three documents. Though the interface became quite slow, we were able to export a PDF of the edition for volumes 1 and 3. Given the higher number of segments in volume 2, we had to splice it into three parts to be able to generate workable editions. We finally read them without cleaning the OCR, referring back to the digital surrogates when necessary. We have attached these five editions of the three texts side-by-side along with a table comparing the chapter titles and how they are numbered in print.

### Initial findings
As there are no structural changes or significant additions, it seems likely Mühlbach simply went through the first edition pen in hand, adjusting as she went along economically, in ways that allowed her to keep most of the text intact. For example, in Book 2, Mühlbach cuts the exchange between General Monk and the protagonist Aphra Behn on the possible dangers of divorce, occurring directly after she asks him to lobby the King of England, in which Behn raises the matter of free love: “_Die Liebe wird um so erhabener sein, je freier sie ist!_” (“Love will be all the more sublime the freer it is”, [vol_2_part_2.pdf](vol_2_part_2.pdf), segments 764–65). In the 1859 edition, the exchange is cut, but replaced with a clause in which Monck casts an anxious glance at his wife. This small addition recontextualises the words already on the page, swapping in comedy (the decorated general is afraid of what his wife will think) for political critique that has gone out of favour. The largest cuts occur in the final chapter of Book 2, in which a final exchange between Behn and her childhood friend Barbara Palmer is removed ([vol_2_part_3.pdf](vol_2_part_3.pdf), segments 2841–3199), and in Book 3, where a full chapter describing a duel is cut ([vol_3.pdf](vol_3.pdf), segments 856–1361). Mühlbach took care to comb through the text line by line, but compositor’s mistakes in the numbering of chapters in both books 2 and 3 (see [Table comparing the book and chapter titles and numbering](#README-for-AB_KZH_chapter_titles_numbering.csv)) suggest that the publication was ultimately hurriedly put together.

As the table shows, the majority of chapter titles are changed in the revised second edition. In fact, all the chapters in Book 1 are modified except for the very first. The general tendency of these changes is to reduce the potential for controversy. Mühlbach avoids political overtones by turning:
- **chapter 1:9:** “_Der Aufstand_” (“The Uprising”) into “_Die Empörung_” (“The Outrage / Rebellion”);
- **chapter 2:2**: “_König und Priester_” (“King and Priest”) into “_Verstellungskünste_” (“Art of Deception”);
- chapter 2:4: “_Der wirkliche König von England_” (“The True King of England”) into “_Der General Monk_”;
- chapter 2:9: “_Der Frauenbund_” (“The Women’s Union”) into “_Die Jugendfreundinnen_” (“The Childhood Female Friends”); and
- chapter 3:4: “_Die Camarilla_” (“The Camarilla/Cronies”) into “_Lady Castlemaine_”.

The changes to the chapter titles also aim to minimize suggestions of impropriety:
- chapter 2:5: “_Die wirkliche Königin des wirklichen Königs von England_” (“The True Queen of the True King of England”) becomes “Die Generalin Monk” (“_Generaless Monk_”);
- chapter 2:13: “_Das freie Weib_” (“The Free Woman”) becomes “_Armuth bezwingt_” (“Poverty Conquers”);
- chapter 3:17: “_Das Nerofest_” (“Nero’s Feast”) becomes “_Der Brand der Flotte_” (“The Fleet on Fire”).

In all, whereas in the 1849 first edition, the chapter titles gave some immediate sense of the novel’s potentially subversive contents, those of the 1859 second edition reveal much less. Moreover, as peritexts, these title changes influence the broader interpretation. For example, titling the chapter (2:13) where Behn decides to work “Poverty Conquers” (1859) rather than “The Free Woman” (1849) increases the suggestion of her victimhood, as it tilts interpretation of her motivation toward economic necessity rather than her desire for independence. This retitling is part of a broader depoliticisation of women’s significance for the novel’s historical plot, seen also in the aforementioned shifts from “The Women’s Union” to “The Childhood Female Friends” (2:9) and from “The True Queen of the True King of England”, indicating Lady Monk’s oblique influence over matters of state, to “Generaless Monck” (2:5), a more straightforward quip about her mannishness.

## ID column
The table requires sequential chapter numbers because, though in the first book they are identical to those printed in both editions, there are errors in the second book within chapter numbers printed in both editions. Moreover, in the third and last book of the revised second edition, one chapter is cut out, another is split in two, and there are further chapter numbering errors.

`Sequential book and chapter number` Standardized sequential numbering of the books and the chapters they contain in the format `[b]`:`[c]`, in which `[b]` is the number of the book (1 to 3) and `[c]` is a sequential number for the chapter
	
## Book and chapter titles and numbering

## Collation PDFs
This folder also contains the collation of _Aphra Behn. Roman_ (Berlin: M. Simion, 1849) and _Karl der Zweite und sein Hof. Historischer Roman_ (Berlin: Otto Janke, 1859) in five PDFs generated by the digital tool Locate, Explore, Retrace and Apprehend complex text variants, or [LERA](https://lera.uzi.uni-halle.de/). There is one PDF for each volume except the second which, being larger, slowed down the tool too much. The text file of the second volume was thus split into three parts.

In the column names below, `*` represents `1`, `2-1`, `2-2`, `2-3`, or `3`, depending on the PDF containing the chapter as indicated in column `Filename` (`vol_1`, `vol_2_part_1`, `vol_2_part_2`, `vol_2_part_3`, or `vol_3`).

`Filename`	Filename of the PDF containing the chapter

`ABb*e1744 segment no for numbering`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's numbering appears in the copy identified as ABb*e1744, first column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"

`ABb*e1744 segment no for title`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's title appears in the copy identified as ABb*e1744, first column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"

`ABb*e958 segment no for numbering`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's numbering appears in the copy identified as ABb*e958, second column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"

`ABb*e958 segment no for title`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's title appears in the copy identified as ABb*e958, second column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"

`KZHb* segment no for numbering`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's numbering appears in the copy identified as KZHb*, third and last column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"

`KZHb* segment no for title`  Number of the segment (as indicated on the left-hand side of the collation PDF) in which each book and chapter's title appears in the copy identified as KZHb1, third and last column of text in the file specified in the field "Filename"
